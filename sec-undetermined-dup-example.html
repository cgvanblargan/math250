    Section 3.5.4 Example with the Case of Duplication

    Recall from Theorem 3.5.2 that when the ODE has the form
    \begin{equation*} y'' + k^2 y = m_1\cos(kt) + m_2\sin(kt), \end{equation*}
    the usual guess \(y_p = A\cos(kt) + B\sin(kt)\) fails because it solves the homogeneous equation. In this case we use the modified guess
    \begin{equation*} y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(kt) + B\sin(kt)\bigr). \end{equation*}
    Let’s work through a full example.
    🔗

    Example 3.5.1. Finding a Particular Solution in the Case of Duplication.

    Find a particular solution to
    \begin{equation*} y'' + 9y = 12\cos(3t) + 16\sin(3t). \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a) Recognizing the case of duplication.

    Notice that the coefficient of \(y\) is \(9\text{,}\) and the right-hand side has frequency \(k = 3\text{.}\) Since \(k^2 = 9\) matches the coefficient of \(y\text{,}\) we are in the case of duplication. What is the correct guess for \(y_p\text{?}\)
    🔗
    • \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\bigr)\)
    • Correct! Since we are in the case of duplication, we multiply the usual guess by \(t\text{.}\) \(\checkmark\)
    • \(y_p = A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\)
    • This is the usual guess, but we just established we are in the case of duplication. This guess solves \(y'' + 9y = 0\text{,}\) so substituting it in gives \(0\) on the left, not \(12\cos(3t) + 16\sin(3t)\text{.}\)
    • \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(9t) + B\sin(9t)\bigr)\)
    • The frequency in the guess should match the right-hand side, which has \(k = 3\text{,}\) not \(k = 9\text{.}\)
    • \(y_p = t^2\bigl(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\bigr)\)
    • Multiplying by \(t\) is enough to fix the duplication. We only need \(t^2\) in more advanced cases not covered here.
    🔗

    (b) Finding \(A\) and \(B\).

    Use the guess \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\bigr)\text{.}\) Substitute into \(y'' + 9y = 12\cos(3t) + 16\sin(3t)\) and solve for \(A\) and \(B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Using the product rule:
    • \(\displaystyle y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\bigr)\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle y_p' = \bigl(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\bigr) + t\bigl(-3A\sin(3t) + 3B\cos(3t)\bigr)\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle y_p'' = \bigl(6B\cos(3t) - 6A\sin(3t)\bigr) + t\bigl(-9A\cos(3t) - 9B\sin(3t)\bigr)\)
      🔗
      🔗
    Plug these into \(y'' + 9y\) and solve for \(A\) and \(B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    We have:
    • \(\displaystyle y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\bigr)\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle y_p' = \bigl(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\bigr) + t\bigl(-3A\sin(3t) + 3B\cos(3t)\bigr)\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle y_p'' = ?? \)
      🔗
      🔗
    Solution 1. Computing \(y_p''\) via product rule
    Differentiating \(y_p'\) with the product rule:
    \begin{align*} y_p'' \amp= \bigl(-3A\sin(3t) + 3B\cos(3t)\bigr)\\ \amp\quad + \bigl(-3A\sin(3t) + 3B\cos(3t)\bigr)\\ \amp\quad + t\bigl(-9A\cos(3t) - 9B\sin(3t)\bigr)\\ \amp= \bigl(6B\cos(3t) - 6A\sin(3t)\bigr) + t\bigl(-9A\cos(3t) - 9B\sin(3t)\bigr). \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    So:
    \begin{equation*} y_p'' = \underbrace{\bigl(6B\cos(3t) - 6A\sin(3t)\bigr)}_{\text{terms without }t} + \textcolor{blue}{\underbrace{t\bigl(-9A\cos(3t) - 9B\sin(3t)\bigr)}_{\text{terms with }t}}. \end{equation*}
    Now substituting into \(y'' + 9y\text{:}\)
    Solution 2. Substituting and cancelling the \(t\) terms
    \begin{align*} y_p'' + 9y_p \amp= \underbrace{6B\cos(3t) - 6A\sin(3t)}_{\text{terms from }y_p''\text{ without }t} + \textcolor{blue}{\underbrace{t(-9A\cos(3t) - 9B\sin(3t))}_{\text{terms from }y_p''\text{ with }t}}\\ \amp\quad + \textcolor{blue}{\underbrace{9t(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t))}_{\text{all terms from }9y_p}}. \end{align*}
    The \(\textcolor{blue}{t}\) terms cancel:
    \begin{equation*} \textcolor{blue}{-9At\cos(3t) - 9Bt\sin(3t) + 9At\cos(3t) + 9Bt\sin(3t) = 0.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    After cancellation, our ODE becomes:
    \begin{equation*} \underbrace{6B\cos(3t) - 6A\sin(3t)}_{y_p''+9y_p \text{ after cancellation}} = \underbrace{12\cos(3t) + 16\sin(3t)}_{\text{original RHS}}. \end{equation*}
    Now we solve for \(A\) and \(B\text{:}\)
    Solution 3. Solving for \(A\) and \(B\)
    Matching coefficients in
    \begin{equation*} \textcolor{red}{6B\cos(3t)} \textcolor{blue}{- 6A\sin(3t)} = \textcolor{red}{12\cos(3t)} + \textcolor{blue}{16\sin(3t)}: \end{equation*}
    \begin{align*} \textcolor{blue}{-6A} \amp\textcolor{blue}{= 16} \amp\implies A \amp= -\frac{8}{3}\\ \textcolor{red}{6B} \amp\textcolor{red}{= 12} \amp\implies B \amp= 2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    So \(A = -\dfrac{8}{3}\) and \(B = 2\text{,}\) giving the particular solution:
    \begin{equation*} y_p = t\!\left(\underbrace{-\frac{8}{3}}_{A}\cos(3t) + \underbrace{2}_{B}\sin(3t)\right). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Notice what happened with the \(t\) terms when we substituted in. In this example, the terms with \(t\) were \(t(-9A\cos(3t) - 9B\sin(3t))\) from \(y_p''\) and \(9t(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t))\) from \(9y_p\text{.}\) These cancelled exactly because \(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\) solves the homogeneous equation \(y'' + 9y = 0\text{,}\) which is precisely the definition of the case of duplication. This is not a coincidence: the \(t\) terms will always cancel when we are in the case of duplication, for exactly this reason. So in practice, once you compute \(y_p''\text{,}\) you can immediately drop the \(t\) terms and just work with what remains.
    🔗

    Key Takeaways.

    • Recognizing duplication: Check whether \(k^2\) matches the coefficient of \(y\) in the ODE. If so, use the modified guess \(y_p = t(A\cos(kt) + B\sin(kt))\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • The \(t\) terms always cancel: When substituting the modified guess into the ODE, the terms with a factor of \(t\) will always cancel out. You are left with only plain \(\cos(kt)\) and \(\sin(kt)\) terms to match against the right-hand side.
