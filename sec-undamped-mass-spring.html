    Section 3.2.1 The Undamped Mass-Spring System

    Alright, so we now have a full understanding of how to solve the ODE
    \begin{equation*} ay''+by'+cy'=0. \end{equation*}
    This ODE turns out to have a very interesting application to the mass-spring model. The idea behind the model is simple: a block of mass \(m\) is attached to a spring, and we want to track its position over time.
    🔗
    Press "Play" on the animation below to get a feel for how the mass moves. The mass oscillates back and forth between a compressed state and a stretched state, passing through the equilibrium position each time. The equilibrium position is simply where the spring sits at rest with no velocity: if the mass starts there undisturbed, it stays there. But if it starts displaced in either direction, the spring pulls it back, and the oscillation begins.
    🔗
    Borrowing some facts from physics, we will give a brief derivation of how to obtain an ODE modelling the system. Everything below follows from just Hooke’s Law and Newton’s second law of motion. Later on we will mostly be focusing on interpreting the behavior of solutions to the ODE and how it relates to the physical reality.
    🔗

    Fact 3.2.2. Hooke’s Law.

    The restoring force exerted by a spring is proportional to the displacement \(x(t)\) from equilibrium:
    \begin{equation*} F_s = -kx, \end{equation*}
    where \(k \gt 0\) is the spring constant. The negative sign means the force always pulls the mass back toward equilibrium.
    🔗
    🔗
    By Newton’s second law, the total force on the mass equals \(ma\text{,}\) where \(a = x''(t)\) is the acceleration. Since the only force acting on the mass is the spring force \(F_s\text{,}\) we have:
    \begin{equation*} mx'' = F_s = -kx. \end{equation*}
    Rearranging gives us the undamped mass-spring ODE:
    \begin{equation*} mx'' + kx = 0. \end{equation*}
    We can simplify this by dividing through by \(m\) and letting \(\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} x'' + \omega_0^2 x = 0. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Let’s give a quick summary of this below.
    🔗

    Definition 3.2.3. Undamped Mass Spring System.

    The undamped mass-spring ODE is:
    \begin{equation*} mx'' + kx = 0, \end{equation*}
    where \(m\) is the mass of the object on the spring and \(k\) is the spring constant in the system. Dividing through by \(m\text{,}\) we can write this as
    \begin{equation*} x'' + \omega_0^2 x = 0, \end{equation*}
    where the constant \(\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}\) is called the natural frequency of the system. It depends only on the spring constant and the mass.
    🔗
    This ODE describes free undamped motion. It is free because there is no external driving force, and undamped because there is no friction or damping force slowing the mass down.
    🔗
    🔗
    Notice that \(x'' + \omega_0^2 x = 0\) is exactly in the form we saw in Observation 3.1.2. The general solution is:
    \begin{equation*} x(t) = c_1\cos(\omega_0 t) + c_2\sin(\omega_0 t). \end{equation*}
    This is called simple harmonic motion: the mass oscillates back and forth forever, with the shape of the solution determined by the initial conditions \(x(0)\) and \(x'(0)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Remark 3.2.4. An Equivalent Form.

    The solution \(x(t) = c_1\cos(\omega_0 t) + c_2\sin(\omega_0 t)\) can also be written as a single cosine function:
    \begin{equation*} x(t) = A\cos(\omega_0(t - \delta)), \end{equation*}
    where \(A = \sqrt{c_1^2 + c_2^2}\) is the amplitude (the maximum displacement from equilibrium) and \(\delta\) is a phase shift. We will skip the proof, but see the dropdown if you are curious.
    🔗

    Why these two forms are equivalent (can skim).

    Using the cosine angle subtraction formula:
    \begin{equation*} A\cos(\omega_0(t - \delta)) = A\cos(\omega_0 t)\cos(\delta) + A\sin(\omega_0 t)\sin(\delta). \end{equation*}
    Setting \(c_1 = A\cos(\delta)\) and \(c_2 = A\sin(\delta)\text{,}\) we get \(c_1^2 + c_2^2 = A^2(\cos^2\delta + \sin^2\delta) = A^2\text{,}\) so \(A = \sqrt{c_1^2 + c_2^2}\text{.}\) The two forms are identical.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Okay, so we covered a lot of ground on this page! There was quite a bit of set-up with the physics, but we will start getting more familiar with all these terms as we work through more examples. Let’s summarize below:
    🔗

    Key Takeaways.

    • The ODE: A mass \(m\) on a spring with constant \(k\) satisfies \(mx'' + kx = 0\text{,}\) or equivalently \(x'' + \omega_0^2 x = 0\) where \(\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Simple harmonic motion: The general solution is \(x(t) = c_1\cos(\omega_0 t) + c_2\sin(\omega_0 t)\text{,}\) or equivalently \(A\cos(\omega_0(t - \delta))\) with amplitude \(A = \sqrt{c_1^2 + c_2^2}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Free undamped: No external force, no friction. The mass oscillates forever. This changes when we add damping next week!
