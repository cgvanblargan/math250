    Exercises 3.4.6 Exercises for Section 3.4

    In everything below: when \(x(t) \gt 0\) the spring is stretched; when \(x(t) \lt 0\) it is compressed; and \(x = 0\) is the equilibrium position. Each problem has a lot of overlap with techniques from the subsections, so make sure to reference similar examples when stuck! There are some hints available, but think about it on your own before looking at them.
    🔗

    1. Analyzing an Underdamped System.

    A mass-spring-dashpot system has \(m = 1\text{,}\) \(c = 2\text{,}\) \(k = 5\text{,}\) with initial conditions \(x(0) = -1\) and \(x'(0) = 1\text{.}\) I will do the first part for you and provide you with the specific solution to this IVP:
    \begin{equation*} x(t) = -e^{-t}\cos(2t). \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Does the mass start stretched or compressed? Which direction does it initially move?
    🔗
    Hint.
    The values \(x(0)\) and \(x'(0)\) were already given in the problem statement above.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Give a rough sketch of \(x(t)\) based on part (a) and what you know about the general behavior of underdamped solutions.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find all times \(t \geq 0\) at which the mass changes direction.
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    When is the mass most stretched? When is it most compressed?
    🔗
    Hint.
    Direction changes occur at critical points of \(x(t)\text{.}\) For an underdamped system, critical points alternate between local maxima (most stretched at that moment) and local minima (most compressed at that moment). Because the amplitude is always decaying, the overall most stretched position occurs at the first local maximum, and the overall most compressed position occurs at the first local minimum. Use your graph from (b) to determine which comes first (local max or local min), then use your answer from part (c): the first two values of \(n\) correspond to the two answers.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    In a sentence or two, describe what happens to the amplitude of the oscillations over time.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Analyzing an Overdamped System.

    The position function of a mass-spring-dashpot system is
    \begin{equation*} x(t) = -4e^{-t} - e^{-5t}. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Compute \(x(0)\) and \(x'(0)\text{.}\) What do these tell you about where the mass starts and which direction it initially moves?
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Does the mass ever pass through the equilibrium position \(x = 0\text{?}\) Justify mathematically.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Solve \(x(t) = 0\) and think carefully about the signs of each term.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Give a rough sketch of \(x(t)\) based on your answers in (a) and (b).
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Is the spring ever stretched? Explain.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Look at your graph from (c). Consider what you know about \(x(0)\) and whether the mass ever crosses equilibrium.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    Describe the full motion of the mass from \(t = 0\) to infinity in words.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3. Analyzing an Overdamped System: Finding Maximum Stretch and Compression.

    A mass-spring-dashpot system has \(m = 1\text{,}\) \(c = 5\text{,}\) \(k = 6\text{,}\) with initial conditions \(x(0) = -2\) and \(x'(0) = 7\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the position function \(x(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Does the mass ever pass through equilibrium \(x = 0\text{?}\) Justify mathematically.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Give a rough sketch of \(x(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Find the time of maximum stretch and maximum compression, or determine that the spring is never stretched or never compressed.
    🔗
    Hint.
    First use your graph and the equilibrium crossing from (b) to determine whether the spring is ever stretched and/or ever compressed. Then recall that maxima and minima occur either at \(t = 0\) or at a critical point where \(x'(t) = 0\text{.}\) Use your graph to determine which case applies for each.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    What does the mass do after crossing the equilibrium position? Describe in words.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Recall the long-term behavior of overdamped solutions. How does this connect to what happens after the crossing? Also consider: which side of equilibrium is the mass on after it crosses?
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4. Analyzing a Critically Damped System.

    A mass-spring-dashpot system has \(m = 1\text{,}\) \(c = 4\text{,}\) \(k = 4\text{,}\) with initial conditions \(x(0) = -1\) and \(x'(0) = -3\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the position function \(x(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    When is the spring most compressed? Is the spring ever stretched? Describe the full motion of the mass after the point of maximum compression.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Work through the following steps:
    • Use \(x(0)\) and \(x'(0)\) to determine where the mass starts and which direction it initially moves.
      🔗
    • Solve \(x(t) = 0\) to check whether the mass ever crosses equilibrium \(x = 0\text{.}\)
      🔗
    • Use these facts to draw a rough sketch of \(x(t)\text{.}\)
      🔗
    • From the sketch, you can determine whether the spring is ever stretched or compressed. Then decide: does the maximum compression occur at \(t = 0\) or at a critical point where \(x'(t) = 0\text{?}\)
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. Analyzing a Critically Damped System: Finding Maximum Stretch.

    The position function of a mass-spring-dashpot system is
    \begin{equation*} x(t) = e^{-2t}(2t - 4). \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Find \(x(0)\) and \(x'(0)\text{,}\) determine whether the mass crosses the equilibrium position \(x = 0\text{,}\) and use this information to give a rough sketch of \(x(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Follow the same steps as in the previous questions: (1) find \(x(0)\) and \(x'(0)\) and interpret them, (2) solve \(x(t) = 0\) to check for equilibrium crossings, (3) use these facts to sketch the graph.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    At what time \(t^*\) is the mass farthest from equilibrium, and what is the position \(x(t^*)\) at that time?
    🔗
    Hint.
    The farthest point from equilibrium occurs either at \(t = 0\) or at a critical point where \(x'(t) = 0\text{.}\) Use your sketch from part (a) to determine which case applies, then solve \(x'(t) = 0\) to find \(t^*\text{.}\) Finally, plug \(t^*\) back into \(x(t)\) to find the actual position.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Describe the motion of the mass after it reaches maximum stretch.
