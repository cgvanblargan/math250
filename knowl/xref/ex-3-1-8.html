Exercise 7. The Discriminant.

Recall the quadratic formula: for \(ar^2 + br + c = 0\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} r = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}. \end{equation*}
The expression under the square root, \(D = b^2 - 4ac\text{,}\) is called the discriminant, and its sign determines which of the three root cases we are in.
🔗

(a)

Without solving for the roots, state which case (two distinct real roots, one repeated real root, or two complex conjugate roots) corresponds to \(D \gt 0\text{,}\) \(D = 0\text{,}\) and \(D \lt 0\text{.}\)

(b)

For the ODE \(y'' + ky' + 9y = 0\text{,}\) find all values of \(k\) for which the general solution involves \(\sin\) and \(\cos\) (i.e., the characteristic equation has complex roots).
🔗
Hint.
The characteristic equation is \(r^2 + kr + 9 = 0\text{,}\) so \(D = k^2 - 36\text{.}\) The roots are complex exactly when \(D \lt 0\text{.}\) Solve this inequality for \(k\text{.}\)
