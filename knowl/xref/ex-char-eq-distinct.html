Example 3.1.6. Solving \(y'' - 5y' + 6y = 0\).

Let’s revisit the ODE from the previous page. This time, instead of being told that \(e^{2x}\) and \(e^{3x}\) are solutions, let’s find them ourselves using the characteristic equation. Find the general solution to \(y'' - 5y' + 6y = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Write down the characteristic equation by replacing \(y''\) with \(r^2\text{,}\) \(y'\) with \(r\text{,}\) and \(y\) with \(1\text{.}\) Then factor and solve for \(r\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The characteristic equation is:
\begin{equation*} r^2 - 5r + 6 = 0. \end{equation*}
Factoring: \((r - 2)(r - 3) = 0\text{,}\) so \(r_1 = 2\) and \(r_2 = 3\text{.}\) These are two distinct real roots, so the general solution is:
\begin{equation*} y = c_1 e^{2x} + c_2 e^{3x}. \end{equation*}
Just as we found on the previous page!
