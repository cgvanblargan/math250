Theorem 5.5.2. The Eigenvalue Method for Complex Conjugate Eigenvalues.

To solve \(\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = A\mathbf{x}\) when \(A\) has complex conjugate eigenvalues \(\lambda_1, \lambda_2\) with corresponding eigenvectors \(\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2\text{:}\)
  1. Write down the complex-valued solution \(\mathbf{v}_1 e^{\lambda_1 t}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Apply Euler’s formula and separate into real and imaginary parts:
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{v}_1 e^{\lambda_1 t} = \mathbf{y} + i\mathbf{z}, \end{equation*}
    where \(\mathbf{y}\) and \(\mathbf{z}\) contain no \(i\) at all. Then it must be that \(\mathbf{y}\) and \(\mathbf{z}\) are themselves real-valued solutions, and they are linearly independent.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The general solution (for all real-valued solutions) is
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{x}(t) = c_1\mathbf{y} + c_2\mathbf{z}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
