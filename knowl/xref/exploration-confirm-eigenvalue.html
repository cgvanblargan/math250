Exploration 5.4.1. Confirming an Eigenvalue.

Suppose someone claims that \(\lambda = -2\) is an eigenvalue of \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \amp 2 \\ 3 \amp -1 \end{bmatrix}\) with eigenvector \(\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) How would we confirm that? Recall that the definition says to check whether \(A\mathbf{v} = \lambda \mathbf{v}\) for some number \(\lambda\text{.}\)
🔗

(a) Compute \(A\mathbf{v}\).

Using the matrix-vector product from earlier in the chapter, compute:
\begin{equation*} A\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \amp 2 \\ 3 \amp -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
  • \(A\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}\)
  • Correct! The first component is \(4 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot (-3) = 4 - 6 = -2\text{,}\) and the second is \(3 \cdot 1 + (-1)(-3) = 3 + 3 = 6\text{.}\)
  • \(A\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}\)
  • Watch the signs! The second component of \(\mathbf{v}\) is \(-3\text{,}\) not \(3\text{.}\) The first component of \(A\mathbf{v}\) should be \(4 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot (-3) = 4 - 6 = -2\text{.}\)
  • \(A\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\)
  • The first component is right, but check the second: it uses the bottom row of \(A\text{,}\) giving \(3 \cdot 1 + (-1)(-3) = 3 + 3 = 6\text{.}\) The two negatives multiply to a positive.
🔗

(b) Compare \(A\mathbf{v}\) to \(\mathbf{v}\).

From the previous task, \(A\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Now check whether it is a scalar multiple of the vector \(\mathbf{v}\) we started with.
🔗
Solution. Verifying whether v is an eigenvector
Yes! It is a scalar multiple of the vector we started with:
\begin{equation*} A\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{-2}_{\lambda}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix} = -2\mathbf{v}. \end{equation*}
This is exactly the eigenvalue equation \(A\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}\) with \(\lambda = -2\text{.}\) Notice that both components scaled by the same factor: \(1 \mapsto -2\) and \(-3 \mapsto 6\) are each "multiply by \(-2\text{.}\)"
🔗
🔗
🔗
This computation confirms that \(\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}\) is an eigenvector of \(A\text{,}\) with an eigenvalue of \(\lambda = -2\text{.}\)
