Example 3.1.3. Solving \(y'' + 4y' + 5y = 0\).

Find the general solution to \(y'' + 4y' + 5y = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Notice the characteristic equation is \(r^2 + 4r + 5 = 0\text{.}\) This does not factor easily, so use the quadratic formula:
\begin{equation*} r = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}. \end{equation*}
You should find complex roots in this case!
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The characteristic equation is \(r^2 + 4r + 5 = 0\text{.}\) Applying the quadratic formula with \(a = 1\text{,}\) \(b = 4\text{,}\) \(c = 5\text{:}\)
\begin{align*} r \amp= \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 20}}{2}\\ \amp= \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{-4}}{2}\\ \amp= \frac{-4 \pm 2i}{2}\\ \amp= -2 \pm i. \end{align*}
So we have complex roots with \(\alpha = -2\) and \(\beta = 1\text{.}\) By the theorem, the two linearly independent solutions are:
\begin{equation*} y_1 = e^{-2x}\cos(x) \qquad \text{and} \qquad y_2 = e^{-2x}\sin(x). \end{equation*}
So the general solution is:
\begin{equation*} y = e^{-2x}\left(c_1\cos(x) + c_2\sin(x)\right). \end{equation*}
