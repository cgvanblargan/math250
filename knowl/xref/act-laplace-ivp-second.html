Activity 4.3.1. Solve the IVP \(y'' + 9y = 9\text{,}\) \(y(0) = 1\text{,}\) \(y'(0) = -3\).

Work through the three steps to solve this initial value problem using the Laplace transform.
🔗

(a) Step 1a: Substitute the Derivative Formulas.

Taking \(\mathcal{L}\) of both sides and applying linearity on the left and Formula A on the right:
\begin{equation*} \mathcal{L}\{y''\} + 9\,\mathcal{L}\{y\} = \frac{9}{s}. \end{equation*}
Using the derivative formulas from Theorem 4.3.1 with \(y(0) = 1\) and \(y'(0) = -3\text{,}\) which of the following is the correct result?
🔗
  • \(\bigl(s^2 Y(s) - s + 3\bigr) + 9Y(s) = \dfrac{9}{s}\)
  • Correct! Substituting \(\mathcal{L}\{y''\} = s^2Y(s) - s(1) - (-3) = s^2Y(s) - s + 3\) and \(\mathcal{L}\{y\} = Y(s)\) gives exactly this.
  • \(\bigl(s^2 Y(s) - s - 3\bigr) + 9Y(s) = \dfrac{9}{s}\)
  • Check the sign of \(y'(0)\text{.}\) The formula gives \(-sy(0) - y'(0) = -s - (-3) = -s + 3\text{,}\) so the constant should be \(+3\text{,}\) not \(-3\text{.}\)
  • \(\bigl(s^2 Y(s) - 2s - 1\bigr) + 9Y(s) = \dfrac{9}{s}\)
  • It looks like the wrong initial conditions were used. Here \(y(0) = 1\) and \(y'(0) = -3\text{,}\) so \(-sy(0) - y'(0) = -s + 3\text{.}\)
  • \(\bigl(s^2 Y(s) + s + 3\bigr) + 9Y(s) = \dfrac{9}{s}\)
  • The term \(-sy(0) = -s(1) = -s\text{,}\) not \(+s\text{.}\) Check the sign in the derivative formula.
🔗

(b) Step 1b: Solve for \(Y(s)\).

Using the result from the previous part, \(\bigl(s^2 Y(s) - s + 3\bigr) + 9Y(s) = \dfrac{9}{s}\text{,}\) which of the following is the correct expression for \(Y(s)\text{?}\)
🔗
  • \(Y(s) = \dfrac{9}{s(s^2+9)} + \dfrac{s}{s^2+9} - \dfrac{3}{s^2+9}\)
  • Correct! Collecting \(Y(s)\) terms gives \((s^2+9)Y(s) = \frac{9}{s} + s - 3\text{,}\) and dividing by \((s^2+9)\) gives this expression.
  • \(Y(s) = \dfrac{9}{s(s^2+9)} - \dfrac{s}{s^2+9} + \dfrac{3}{s^2+9}\)
  • Check the signs when moving terms to the right side. \(-s + 3\) moves to the right as \(+s - 3\text{,}\) not \(-s + 3\text{.}\)
  • \(Y(s) = \dfrac{9+s-3}{s^2+9}\)
  • The first term \(\frac{9}{s}\) on the right side needs to keep its \(s\) in the denominator when dividing by \((s^2+9)\text{.}\) It becomes \(\frac{9}{s(s^2+9)}\text{.}\)
  • \(Y(s) = \dfrac{9}{s} + \dfrac{s-3}{s^2+9}\)
  • Don’t forget to divide every term by \((s^2+9)\text{.}\) The \(\frac{9}{s}\) term becomes \(\frac{9}{s(s^2+9)}\text{,}\) not just \(\frac{9}{s}\text{.}\)
🔗

(c) Step 2: Partial Fraction Decomposition.

Dividing by \((s^2+9)\) gives:
\begin{equation*} Y(s) = \frac{9}{s(s^2+9)} + \frac{s}{s^2+9} - \frac{3}{s^2+9}. \end{equation*}
The first term needs a partial fraction decomposition since it involves a linear factor and an irreducible quadratic, as in Section 4.2.3. The decomposition gives:
\begin{equation*} \frac{9}{s(s^2+9)} = \frac{1}{s} - \frac{s}{s^2+9}. \end{equation*}
Solution. Solving for the PFD coefficients (can skim, reference for homework)
The correct form is \(\dfrac{9}{s(s^2+9)} = \dfrac{A}{s} + \dfrac{Bs+C}{s^2+9}\text{.}\) Clearing denominators:
\begin{equation*} 9 = A(s^2+9) + (Bs+C)s. \end{equation*}
Expanding and matching coefficients:
\begin{align*} \underbrace{0}_{\text{coeff of }s^2} \cdot s^2 + \underbrace{0}_{\text{coeff of }s} \cdot s + \underbrace{9}_{\text{const}} \amp= \underbrace{(A+B)}_{\text{coeff of }s^2}s^2 + \underbrace{C}_{\text{coeff of }s}s + \underbrace{9A}_{\text{const}}. \end{align*}
So \(A+B = 0\text{,}\) \(C = 0\text{,}\) \(9A = 9\text{,}\) giving \(A = 1\text{,}\) \(B = -1\text{,}\) \(C = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Substituting back into the expression for \(Y(s) \text{:}\)
\begin{align*} Y(s) \amp= \overbrace{\frac{1}{s} - \frac{s}{s^2+9}}^{\text{result of the PFD}} + \frac{s}{s^2+9} - \frac{3}{s^2+9}\\ Y(s) \amp= \frac{1}{s} - \frac{3}{s^2+9}. \end{align*}
🔗
🔗

(d) Step 3: Apply the Inverse Laplace Transform.

Apply \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\) to \(Y(s) = \dfrac{1}{s} - \dfrac{3}{s^2+9}\) to find \(y(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
\(\dfrac{1}{s}\) matches Formula A. For \(\dfrac{3}{s^2+9}\text{,}\) notice \(s^2 + 9 = s^2 + 3^2\) and the 3 is already in the numerator, so it matches Formula D with \(a = 3\) directly, no rewriting needed.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Applying linearity and reading off the table:
\begin{align*} y(t) \amp= \mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{ \underbrace{\frac{1}{s}}_{\text{Formula A}} \right\} - \mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{ \underbrace{\frac{3}{s^2+3^2}}_{\text{Formula D},\, a=3} \right\}\\ \amp= 1 - \sin(3t). \end{align*}
