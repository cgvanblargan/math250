    Section 3.4.2 Analyzing Overdamped and Critically Damped Motion

    Overdamped and critically damped systems share the same qualitative behavior: the mass returns to equilibrium without oscillating, passing through it at most once.
    🔗

    Summary: Overdamped and Critically Damped Cases of \(mx'' + cx' + kx = 0\).

    Case Discriminant and roots Crossings of \(x=0\) \(\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty} x(t)\)
    Overdamped (larger \(c\)) \(c^2 > 4km\) (two distinct real roots) At most once \(0\)
    Critically damped (borderline) \(c^2 = 4km\) (one repeated real root) At most once \(0\)
    🔗
    For the rest of this page we analyze the position function
    \begin{equation*} x(t) = 4e^{-2t} - 10e^{-t}. \end{equation*}
    We will use information about this function to build a complete picture of the motion.
    🔗

    Exploration 3.4.1. Building a Picture of the Motion.

    Throughout this exploration, \(x(t) = 4e^{-2t} - 10e^{-t}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a) Which Damping Case?

    Based on the form of \(x(t) = 4e^{-2t} - 10e^{-t}\text{,}\) which damping case are we in?
    🔗
    • Overdamped
    • Correct! The solution is a sum of two distinct decaying exponentials, \(x(t) = c_1 e^{r_1 t} + c_2 e^{r_2 t}\) with \(r_1 = -2\) and \(r_2 = -1\text{.}\) Two distinct real roots means \(c^2 > 4km\text{,}\) which is the overdamped case. \(\checkmark\)
    • Critically damped
    • Not quite. A critically damped solution has the form \(x(t) = c_1 e^{-at} + c_2 t e^{-at}\text{,}\) with one exponential and one multiplied by \(t\text{.}\) Here we have two different exponential rates, so the roots are distinct, not repeated.
    • Underdamped
    • Not quite. An underdamped solution has the form \(x(t) = e^{-pt}(c_1\cos(\omega t) + c_2\sin(\omega t))\text{,}\) which involves oscillating trig terms. This solution has no trig functions, so it decays without oscillating.
    🔗

    (b) Does the Mass Pass Through Equilibrium?

    Will the mass ever pass through the equilibrium position \(x = 0\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Set \(x(t) = 0\) and solve for \(t\text{.}\) Remember that only \(t \geq 0\) is physically meaningful.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Full solution
    We solve \(4e^{-2t} - 10e^{-t} = 0\text{.}\) Since \(e^{-t} \neq 0\text{,}\) divide both sides by \(e^{-t}\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} 4e^{-t} - 10 = 0 \implies e^{-t} = \frac{10}{4} = \frac{5}{2} \implies t = -\ln\!\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right) \approx -0.92. \end{equation*}
    Since \(t \lt 0\text{,}\) this is not a valid time. The mass never passes through equilibrium for \(t \geq 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c) Initial Position and Velocity.

    Compute \(x(0)\) and \(x'(0)\text{.}\) What do these tell you about where the mass starts and which direction it initially moves?
    🔗
    Hint.
    Recall: \(x(0) > 0\) means stretched, \(x(0) \lt 0\) means compressed. \(x'(0) > 0\) means moving in the positive direction (toward equilibrium when compressed), \(x'(0) \lt 0\) means moving in the negative direction.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Work
    Initial position:
    \begin{equation*} x(0) = 4 - 10 = -6. \end{equation*}
    Since \(x(0) = -6 \lt 0\text{,}\) the mass starts compressed.
    🔗
    Initial velocity: Differentiating,
    \begin{equation*} x'(t) = -8e^{-2t} + 10e^{-t}, \end{equation*}
    so \(x'(0) = -8 + 10 = 2 > 0\text{.}\) The mass is initially moving in the positive direction, that is, toward the equilibrium position.
    🔗
    Together: the mass starts compressed at \(x = -6\text{,}\) moves toward equilibrium, but (from Task 2) never reaches it. So the mass stays compressed for all \(t \geq 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d) Sketch the Graph.

    From Task 3 we observed that \(x(0) = -6\) and \(x'(0) = 2 > 0\text{,}\) so:
    • \(x(0) \lt 0\text{:}\) the mass starts compressed, and
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(x'(0) = 2 > 0\text{:}\) it is initially moving in the positive direction.
      🔗
      🔗
    Using this, together with the fact that the mass never crosses equilibrium (Task 2), determine which graph below matches \(x(t) = 4e^{-2t} - 10e^{-t}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    • Graph (a)
    • Graph (a) does start compressed, which is correct. However, it then moves further from equilibrium before turning around, meaning \(x'(0) \lt 0\) initially. But we found \(x'(0) = 2 > 0\text{,}\) so the mass is moving toward equilibrium from the very start, not away from it.
    • Graph (b)
    • Correct! Graph (b) starts below zero (compressed), moves in the positive direction toward equilibrium, and never crosses \(x = 0\text{,}\) exactly matching what we found in Tasks 2 and 3. \(\checkmark\)
    • Graph (c)
    • Graph (c) shows oscillations, which is the behavior of an underdamped system. But \(x(t) = 4e^{-2t} - 10e^{-t}\) has two distinct real exponential terms with no trig functions, so it is overdamped and does not oscillate.
    🔗
    🔗
    The key idea here was using the following information:
    1. the sign of \(x(0)\) (does it start compressed, stretched, or at equilibrium?),
      🔗
      🔗
    2. the sign of \(x'(0)\) (is it initially moving left or right?), and
      🔗
      🔗
    3. whether the mass ever crosses the equilibrium position.
      🔗
      🔗
    These three pieces of information will always allow you to give a sketch of the graph, as we did in part (c).
    🔗
    Now that we have a clear picture of the motion, let’s use what we found to answer some more specific questions about the spring’s behavior.
    🔗

    Example 3.4.2. Analyzing \(x(t) = 4e^{-2t} - 10e^{-t}\).

    (a) Will the mass pass through equilibrium?

    Hint.
    This was solved in Task 3.4.1.b of the exploration.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    From Task 3.4.1.b: the only solution to \(x(t) = 0\) occurs at \(t = -\ln(5/2) \approx -0.92 \lt 0\text{,}\) which is not a valid time. The mass never passes through equilibrium.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b) When is the spring most stretched?

    Hint.
    What can you deduce from the sketch of the graph in the exploration? Think about what the initial conditions and the equilibrium crossing result together tell you about whether the spring is ever stretched.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    From the exploration: the mass starts compressed (\(x(0) = -6 \lt 0\)) and never crosses the equilibrium position. Therefore \(x(t) \lt 0\) for all \(t \geq 0\text{,}\) so the spring is never stretched. This is confirmed by the graph below, which stays entirely below \(x = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c) When is the spring most compressed?

    Hint.
    What can you deduce from the sketch of the graph that used knowledge of \(x(0)\) and \(x'(0)\text{?}\) Think about the direction of motion from the very start.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    From the exploration: \(x(0) = -6\) and \(x'(0) = 2 > 0\text{,}\) so the mass starts compressed and is already moving toward equilibrium at \(t = 0\text{.}\) Since it never crosses equilibrium, it moves closer to zero for all \(t > 0\text{.}\) Therefore \(x(0) = -6\) is the most negative value, and the spring is most compressed at \(t = 0\). The graph confirms this: the curve starts at its lowest point and increases from there.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaways.

    To sketch the graph of \(x(t)\) and answer questions about the motion, we should begin by finding:
    1. The sign of \(x(0)\text{:}\) does the mass start compressed, stretched, or at equilibrium?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. The sign of \(x'(0)\text{:}\) which direction does the mass initially move?
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Whether the mass ever crosses equilibrium: for overdamped and critically damped systems, this happens at most once.
      🔗
      🔗
    These three pieces of information are enough to sketch the graph of \(x(t)\text{,}\) and from that sketch we can answer qualitative questions about the behavior of the system.
