    Section 3.4.3 Analyzing an Overdamped System: Is the Spring Ever Stretched?

    In Section 3.4.2 we used the sign of \(x(0)\text{,}\) the sign of \(x'(0)\text{,}\) and whether the mass crosses equilibrium to build a complete picture of the motion. In this section, we put that same approach to work on a new question: for the system below, is the spring ever stretched? We’ll work through an activity to build up the picture, then come back to this question at the end.
    🔗
    We continue analyzing overdamped and critically damped systems. Recall the shared behavior of these two cases.
    🔗

    Summary: Overdamped and Critically Damped Cases of \(mx'' + cx' + kx = 0\).

    Case Discriminant and roots Crossings of \(x=0\) \(\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty} x(t)\)
    Overdamped (larger \(c\)) \(c^2 > 4km\) (two distinct real roots) At most once \(0\)
    Critically damped (borderline) \(c^2 = 4km\) (one repeated real root) At most once \(0\)
    🔗
    Consider the spring-mass-dashpot system with \(m = 1\text{,}\) \(c = 3\text{,}\) \(k = 2\text{,}\) set in motion at \(t = 0\) with \(x(0) = 0\) and \(x'(0) = -2\text{.}\) Substituting these parameters gives the IVP:
    \begin{equation*} x'' + 3x' + 2x = 0, \quad x(0) = 0, \quad x'(0) = -2. \end{equation*}
    The solution to this IVP is:
    \begin{equation*} x(t) = -2e^{-t} + 2e^{-2t}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution. Work: Solution to the IVP (can skim)
    With \(m=1\text{,}\) \(c=3\text{,}\) \(k=2\) the characteristic equation is \(r^2 + 3r + 2 = 0\text{,}\) which factors as \((r+1)(r+2) = 0\text{.}\) This gives two distinct real roots \(r_1 = -1\) and \(r_2 = -2\text{,}\) confirming the system is overdamped. The general solution is:
    \begin{equation*} x(t) = c_1 e^{-t} + c_2 e^{-2t}. \end{equation*}
    Applying \(x(0) = 0\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} c_1 + c_2 = 0 \implies c_2 = -c_1. \end{equation*}
    Differentiating:
    \begin{equation*} x'(t) = -c_1 e^{-t} - 2c_2 e^{-2t}. \end{equation*}
    Applying \(x'(0) = -2\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} -c_1 - 2c_2 = -2 \implies -c_1 + 2c_1 = -2 \implies c_1 = -2. \end{equation*}
    So \(c_1 = -2\text{,}\) \(c_2 = 2\text{,}\) and \(x(t) = -2e^{-t} + 2e^{-2t}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    With that question in mind, let’s work through the following activity to map out the behavior of the system.
    🔗

    Activity 3.4.1. Building a Picture of the Motion.

    Throughout this activity, \(x(t) = -2e^{-t} + 2e^{-2t}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a) Which Damping Case?

    Based on the form of \(x(t) = -2e^{-t} + 2e^{-2t}\text{,}\) which damping case are we in?
    🔗
    • Overdamped
    • Correct! The solution is a sum of two distinct decaying exponentials with roots \(r_1 = -1\) and \(r_2 = -2\text{.}\) Two distinct real roots means \(c^2 > 4km\text{,}\) which is the overdamped case. \(\checkmark\)
    • Critically damped
    • Not quite. A critically damped solution has the form \(x(t) = c_1 e^{-at} + c_2 t e^{-at}\text{,}\) with a repeated exponential rate. Here we have two different rates, so the roots are distinct, not repeated.
    • Underdamped
    • Not quite. An underdamped solution involves oscillating trig terms. This solution has no trig functions, so it decays without oscillating.
    🔗

    (b) Does the Mass Cross the Equilibrium for \(t > 0\text{?}\)

    Does the mass ever cross the equilibrium position \(x = 0\) for \(t > 0\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Set \(x(t) = 0\) and determine whether there are any solutions with \(t > 0\text{.}\) Since \(x(0) = 0\) we already know \(t = 0\) is one solution. We want to know if there are any others.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    We solve \(-2e^{-t} + 2e^{-2t} = 0\text{.}\) Factoring out \(2e^{-2t}\) (which is never zero):
    \begin{equation*} 2e^{-2t}(-e^{t} + 1) = 0 \implies e^{t} = 1 \implies t = 0. \end{equation*}
    The only solution is \(t = 0\text{.}\) The mass never crosses the equilibrium for \(t > 0\text{.}\) Note that the mass does slowly approach zero as \(t \to \infty\text{,}\) but it never actually reaches or crosses it again.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c) Which Way Does the Mass Immediately Move?

    We are given \(x(0) = 0\) and \(x'(0) = -2\text{.}\) The mass starts exactly at the equilibrium position. Based on the initial velocity, does the spring immediately become compressed or stretched?
    🔗
    • Compressed, because \(x'(0) = -2 \lt 0\) means the mass immediately moves in the negative direction.
    • Correct! Since \(x'(0) \lt 0\text{,}\) the mass moves in the negative direction right away, meaning \(x(t) \lt 0\) for small \(t > 0\text{,}\) which is the compressed side. \(\checkmark\)
    • Stretched, because the mass starts at equilibrium and must move somewhere.
    • The initial velocity tells us which direction the mass moves. Since \(x'(0) = -2 \lt 0\text{,}\) it moves in the negative (compressed) direction, not the positive (stretched) direction.
    • We cannot tell from the initial conditions alone.
    • We can tell. The sign of \(x'(0)\) tells us the direction of motion immediately after \(t = 0\text{.}\) Since \(x'(0) = -2 \lt 0\text{,}\) the mass moves in the negative (compressed) direction.
    🔗
    🔗
    Now that we have worked through the activity, we have determined the following:
    • The system is overdamped: no oscillations, the mass decays smoothly toward zero.
      🔗
      🔗
    • The mass starts at equilibrium (\(x(0) = 0\)) and immediately becomes compressed (\(x'(0) = -2 \lt 0\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    • The mass never crosses the equilibrium for \(t > 0\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    Using these three facts, match the graph of \(x(t) = -2e^{-t} + 2e^{-2t}\) below.
    🔗

    (a) Match the Graph.

    Which graph matches \(x(t) = -2e^{-t} + 2e^{-2t}\text{?}\)
    🔗
    • Graph (a)
    • Graph (a) does start at zero and immediately becomes compressed, which is correct. However, it then crosses the equilibrium line and becomes stretched. We showed in the activity that the mass never crosses the equilibrium for \(t > 0\text{,}\) so this cannot be correct.
    • Graph (b)
    • Correct! Graph (b) starts at zero, immediately becomes compressed, reaches a minimum, and then slowly approaches zero from below without ever crossing the equilibrium, exactly matching what we found in the activity. \(\checkmark\)
    • Graph (c)
    • Graph (c) shows oscillations, which is the behavior of an underdamped system. Since \(x(t) = -2e^{-t} + 2e^{-2t}\) has two distinct real exponential terms and no trig functions, the system is overdamped and does not oscillate.
    🔗
    Notice that in graph (b), the mass starts at zero, immediately becomes compressed, and then slowly approaches the equilibrium from below as \(t \to \infty\text{.}\) It gets closer and closer to zero but never actually crosses it again. Since the mass starts at \(x = 0\text{,}\) immediately moves into compressed territory, and never crosses the equilibrium, we can conclude the spring is never stretched for any \(t > 0\text{.}\)
