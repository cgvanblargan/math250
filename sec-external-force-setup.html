    Section 3.6.1 Setting Up the Forced Undamped System

    In Section 3.2.1, we modeled a mass \(m\) attached to a spring with spring constant \(k\text{,}\) with no friction or damping. Newton’s second law gave the free undamped mass-spring ODE:
    \begin{equation*} mx'' + kx = 0, \end{equation*}
    or equivalently, writing \(\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}\) (see Definition 3.2.3),
    \begin{equation*} x'' + \omega_0^2 x = 0. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Before adding anything new, let’s make sure the solution to this equation is fresh. Recall Observation 3.1.2 if you need a reminder.
    🔗

    Checkpoint 3.6.1.

    What is the general solution to \(x'' + \omega_0^2 x = 0\text{?}\)
    🔗
    • \(x = c_1\cos(\omega_0 t) + c_2\sin(\omega_0 t)\)
    • Correct! The characteristic equation is \(r^2 + \omega_0^2 = 0\text{,}\) giving complex roots \(r = \pm\omega_0 i\text{,}\) so the general solution is a combination of cosine and sine at frequency \(\omega_0\text{.}\) \(\checkmark\)
    • \(x = c_1 e^{\omega_0 t} + c_2 e^{-\omega_0 t}\)
    • Exponentials would arise from real roots, but the characteristic equation \(r^2 + \omega_0^2 = 0\) gives \(r = \pm\omega_0 i\) — purely imaginary roots. That means trig functions, not exponentials.
    • \(x = c_1\cos(\omega_0^2 t) + c_2\sin(\omega_0^2 t)\)
    • Close, but the frequency inside the trig functions is \(\omega_0\text{,}\) not \(\omega_0^2\text{.}\) The roots are \(r = \pm\omega_0 i\text{,}\) so the argument is \(\omega_0 t\text{.}\)
    • \(x = c_1\cos(t) + c_2\sin(t)\)
    • This would be the solution to \(x'' + x = 0\) (with \(\omega_0 = 1\)). In general \(\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}\) is not necessarily 1, so the frequency inside the trig functions must be \(\omega_0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    That was free motion: the only forces acting on the mass were the spring restoring force \(F_S = -kx\) and (in the damped case) friction. Now suppose an additional external force \(F_E(t)\) is applied to the mass (think of someone pushing and pulling the block rhythmically). Newton’s second law now reads
    \begin{equation*} ma = F_S + F_E, \end{equation*}
    and since \(F_S = -kx\) and \(a = x''\text{,}\) rearranging gives
    \begin{equation*} mx'' + kx = F_E(t). \end{equation*}
    We focus on the undamped case (no dashpot, so \(c = 0\)), and we take the external force to be sinusoidal:
    \begin{equation*} F_E(t) = F_0\cos(\omega t) + G_0\sin(\omega t), \end{equation*}
    where \(F_0\text{,}\) \(G_0\text{,}\) and \(\omega\) are constants.
    🔗

    Definition 3.6.2. The Forced Undamped Mass-Spring ODE.

    The position \(x(t)\) of an undamped mass-spring system subject to a sinusoidal external force satisfies:
    \begin{equation*} mx'' + kx = F_0\cos(\omega t) + G_0\sin(\omega t). \end{equation*}
    Dividing both sides by \(m\) and writing \(\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} x'' + \omega_0^2 x = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos(\omega t) + \frac{G_0}{m}\sin(\omega t). \end{equation*}
    Here:
    • \(\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}\) is the natural frequency of the system: the frequency it oscillates at when left alone.
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(\omega\) is the forcing frequency: the frequency of the applied external force.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Since this is a non-homogeneous ODE (just like in Section 3.5.6), its general solution will have the form \(x = x_c + x_p\text{,}\) where \(x_p\) is a particular solution and \(x_c\) is the complementary solution: the general solution to the homogeneous equation \(x'' + \omega_0^2 x = 0\text{,}\) i.e., the system with no external force. From the checkpoint above:
    \begin{equation*} x_c = c_1\cos(\omega_0 t) + c_2\sin(\omega_0 t). \end{equation*}
    This is exactly why \(\omega_0\) is called the natural frequency: it is the frequency at which the system freely oscillates on its own, with no outside influence. The forcing frequency \(\omega\text{,}\) by contrast, is imposed from the outside.
    🔗

    Remark 3.6.3. Circular vs. ordinary frequency.

    Technically, \(\omega_0\) and \(\omega\) are circular frequencies, measured in radians per unit time rather than cycles per unit time (which would require a factor of \(2\pi\)). In this context we will simply say “frequency” and mean the circular version throughout.
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaways.

    • The ODE: Adding a sinusoidal external force to the undamped mass-spring system gives \(x'' + \omega_0^2 x = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos(\omega t) + \frac{G_0}{m}\sin(\omega t)\text{,}\) a non-homogeneous ODE we now have the tools to solve.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Two frequencies: \(\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}\) is the natural frequency (how the system oscillates freely); \(\omega\) is the forcing frequency (the rhythm imposed from outside). Whether these are equal or not will turn out to matter enormously.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Complementary solution: \(x_c = c_1\cos(\omega_0 t) + c_2\sin(\omega_0 t)\) comes from the free system, and \(x_p\) will come from the external force via undetermined coefficients. We find \(x_p\) on the next page.
