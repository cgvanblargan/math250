    Section 3.5.5 Practice: Finding the Right Guess for \(y_p\)

    Before we put together a full general solution, let’s make sure we’re comfortable identifying the correct guess for \(y_p\text{.}\) We have two cases to keep in mind, summarized below.
    🔗

    Choosing the Guess for \(y_p\).

    Given \(ay'' + by' + cy = m_1\cos(kt) + m_2\sin(kt)\text{:}\)
    🔗
    • Normal case (from Observation 3.5.2): use the guess \(y_p = A\cos(kt) + B\sin(kt)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Case of duplication (from Theorem 3.5.2): if the ODE can be written as \(y'' + k^2 y = m_1\cos(kt) + m_2\sin(kt)\) (same \(k\) on both sides, no \(y'\) term), use the modified guess \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(kt) + B\sin(kt)\bigr)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    The key step in identifying the case of duplication: write the ODE in standard form (divide through so the leading coefficient is \(1\)) and check whether the coefficient of \(y\) equals \(k^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    The questions below give you practice at just this identification step; you don’t need to solve for \(A\) and \(B\text{,}\) just state the correct form of the guess.
    🔗

    Activity 3.5.1. Identifying the Correct Guess.

    (a) A standard example.

    What is the correct form of the guess \(y_p\) for finding a particular solution to
    \begin{equation*} y'' + 3y' - 10y = 4\cos(6t) - 7\sin(6t)? \end{equation*}
    🔗
    • \(y_p = A\cos(6t) + B\sin(6t)\)
    • Correct! Both terms on the right-hand side share frequency \(k = 6\text{,}\) so the guess uses \(\cos(6t)\) and \(\sin(6t)\text{.}\) The coefficient of \(y\) is \(-10 \neq 36 = k^2\text{,}\) so this is the normal case, no extra \(t\) needed. \(\checkmark\)
    • \(y_p = A\cos(6t) - B\sin(6t)\)
    • The minus sign on the right-hand side doesn’t change the form of the guess. We always write \(y_p = A\cos(6t) + B\sin(6t)\) and let the algebra determine the signs of \(A\) and \(B\) when we solve; one of them may turn out to be negative.
    • \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(6t) + B\sin(6t)\bigr)\)
    • This is the case-of-duplication guess, but we’re not in that case here. Duplication occurs only when the ODE has the form \(y'' + k^2 y = \ldots\) with the same \(k\) on both sides. Here the coefficient of \(y\) is \(-10 \neq 36 = k^2\text{,}\) so we don’t need the extra \(t\text{.}\)
    • \(y_p = A\cos(6t) + B\sin(6t) + C\cos(6t) + D\sin(6t)\)
    • Since both terms on the right have the same frequency, we only need one pair of undetermined coefficients. The guess \(A\cos(6t) + B\sin(6t)\) already has enough flexibility to match both the cosine and sine parts of the right-hand side simultaneously.
    🔗

    (b) A case of duplication.

    What is the correct form of the guess \(y_p\) for finding a particular solution to
    \begin{equation*} y'' + 25y = 3\cos(5t) + 8\sin(5t)? \end{equation*}
    🔗
    • \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(5t) + B\sin(5t)\bigr)\)
    • Correct! Both terms on the right-hand side have frequency \(k = 5\text{,}\) and the coefficient of \(y\) is \(25 = 5^2 = k^2\text{.}\) This is the case of duplication, so we multiply the usual guess by \(t\text{.}\) \(\checkmark\)
    • \(y_p = A\cos(5t) + B\sin(5t)\)
    • This is the usual guess, but it will fail here. The ODE \(y'' + 25y = 0\) has characteristic equation \(r^2 + 25 = 0\text{,}\) giving roots \(r = \pm 5i\text{,}\) so the homogeneous solution is \(c_1\cos(5t) + c_2\sin(5t)\text{.}\) Our guess is already a solution to the homogeneous equation, so plugging it in gives \(0\) on the left, never matching the right-hand side.
    • \(y_p = A\cos(5t)\)
    • Even setting aside the duplication issue, a cosine-only guess won’t work for a right-hand side that includes \(\sin(5t)\text{.}\) And since we’re in the case of duplication, we need the \(t\)-multiplied form as well.
    • \(y_p = t\cdot A\cos(5t)\)
    • We’re in the case of duplication, so we do need the extra \(t\text{,}\) but the full guess must include both \(\cos(5t)\) and \(\sin(5t)\) inside, not just the cosine.
    🔗

    (c) Watch out for a negative coefficient.

    What is the correct form of the guess \(y_p\) for finding a particular solution to
    \begin{equation*} y'' - 9y = 5\cos(3t) - 2\sin(3t)? \end{equation*}
    🔗
    • \(y_p = A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\)
    • Correct! Even though \(k = 3\) and \(3^2 = 9\text{,}\) the coefficient of \(y\) is \(-9\text{,}\) not \(+9\text{.}\) The case of duplication requires the coefficient of \(y\) to be \(+k^2\text{,}\) which means the homogeneous solutions are \(\cos(kt)\) and \(\sin(kt)\text{.}\) Here the characteristic equation is \(r^2 - 9 = 0\text{,}\) giving real roots \(r = \pm 3\) and homogeneous solution \(c_1 e^{3t} + c_2 e^{-3t}\text{,}\) with no trig at all! So the usual guess works fine, with both terms covered by a single pair \(A, B\text{.}\) \(\checkmark\)
    • \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\bigr)\)
    • It’s tempting to see \(k = 3\) and the number \(9 = 3^2\) and call this duplication, but the sign matters! The coefficient of \(y\) is \(-9\text{,}\) not \(+9\text{.}\) The homogeneous equation \(y'' - 9y = 0\) has real exponential solutions \(e^{3t}\) and \(e^{-3t}\text{,}\) not trig functions. The usual guess is not a homogeneous solution, so the extra \(t\) is not needed.
    • \(y_p = Ae^{3t} + Be^{-3t}\)
    • These are the homogeneous solutions, not a form that can match \(5\cos(3t) - 2\sin(3t)\) on the right-hand side. The guess should mirror the form of \(g(t)\text{.}\)
    • \(y_p = A\cos(3t)\)
    • The right-hand side has both a cosine and a sine term, so a cosine-only guess can’t possibly match both. We always need \(y_p = A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\) when \(g(t)\) involves trig at frequency \(k\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (d) Remember to put the ODE in standard form first.

    What is the correct form of the guess \(y_p\) for finding a particular solution to
    \begin{equation*} 2y'' + 18y = \cos(3t) + 4\sin(3t)? \end{equation*}
    🔗
    • \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\bigr)\)
    • Correct! The trick here is to put the ODE in standard form first by dividing through by \(2\text{:}\)
      \begin{equation*} y'' + 9y = \tfrac{1}{2}\cos(3t) + 2\sin(3t). \end{equation*}
      Now the coefficient of \(y\) is \(9 = 3^2 = k^2\text{,}\) so we are in the case of duplication. The modified guess is \(y_p = t(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t))\text{.}\) \(\checkmark\)
    • \(y_p = A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\)
    • This looks right at first glance, but don’t forget to put the ODE in standard form first. Dividing by \(2\) gives \(y'' + 9y = \frac{1}{2}\cos(3t) + 2\sin(3t)\text{,}\) and now \(9 = 3^2 = k^2\text{,}\) so this is the case of duplication. The usual guess will give \(0\) on the left-hand side.
    • \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t)\bigr)\text{,}\) but first replace the right-hand side with \(\cos(3t) + 4\sin(3t)\) since we’ll divide by 2 later.
    • Dividing by \(2\) changes the right-hand side too, giving \(\tfrac{1}{2}\cos(3t) + 2\sin(3t)\text{,}\) not the original. But the form of the guess is otherwise correct: \(y_p = t(A\cos(3t) + B\sin(3t))\text{.}\)
    • \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(18t) + B\sin(18t)\bigr)\)
    • The frequency in the guess always comes from the right-hand side of the ODE. Both \(\cos(3t)\) and \(\sin(3t)\) on the right have frequency \(k = 3\text{.}\) The coefficient \(18\) is from the left-hand side and doesn’t set the frequency.
    🔗

    (e) A subtle one.

    What is the correct form of the guess \(y_p\) for finding a particular solution to
    \begin{equation*} y'' + 4y = \cos(4t) + 3\sin(4t)? \end{equation*}
    🔗
    • \(y_p = A\cos(4t) + B\sin(4t)\)
    • Correct! It’s tempting to see the \(4\) in \(y'' + 4y\) and the \(4t\) on the right and call this duplication, but check carefully. The frequency is \(k = 4\text{,}\) so we need the coefficient of \(y\) to equal \(k^2 = 16\) for duplication. Here the coefficient is only \(4 \neq 16\text{,}\) so this is the normal case. \(\checkmark\)
    • \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(4t) + B\sin(4t)\bigr)\)
    • This is the most natural wrong answer here! The \(4\) in \(y'' + 4y\) and the \(4t\) on the right look like they match, but duplication requires the coefficient of \(y\) to equal \(k^2\text{,}\) not \(k\text{.}\) Since \(k = 4\text{,}\) we need the coefficient to be \(16\text{,}\) not \(4\text{.}\) The homogeneous equation \(y'' + 4y = 0\) has solutions \(\cos(2t)\) and \(\sin(2t)\) (frequency \(2\text{,}\) not \(4\)), so the guess doesn’t duplicate anything.
    • \(y_p = A\cos(2t) + B\sin(2t)\)
    • The frequency in the guess must match the right-hand side, which has frequency \(k = 4\text{.}\) The \(2\) here comes from solving the homogeneous equation \(y'' + 4y = 0\) (whose solutions oscillate at frequency \(\sqrt{4} = 2\)), but that’s not where the guess frequency comes from.
    • \(y_p = t\bigl(A\cos(2t) + B\sin(2t)\bigr)\)
    • Neither the frequency nor the duplication call is right here. The guess frequency should match the right-hand side (\(k = 4\)), and since the coefficient of \(y\) is \(4 \neq 16 = k^2\text{,}\) we’re not in the case of duplication and don’t need the extra \(t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Now that we can reliably identify the right guess, the next step is to use it to build the full general solution: not just a particular solution, but the complete picture including the complementary function.
