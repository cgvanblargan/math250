    Section 5.5.5 Complex Eigenvalues: A Harder Example

    On the last page, the eigenvector \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -i \end{bmatrix}\) had fairly simple entries. In general, the entries of the eigenvector can be full complex numbers like \(2 + i\text{,}\) and multiplying them out takes a little bit more work. The strategy from Theorem 5.5.2 is exactly the same, but there is more involved algebra in step 2.
    🔗
    Before we do the main example on this page, let’s make sure you are comfortable with the relevant complex algebra.
    🔗
    The messy part is multiplying an entry like \(2 + i\) into \(\cos(3t) + i\sin(3t)\text{.}\) Let’s warm up with exactly that computation, in two parts.
    🔗

    Activity 5.5.1. FOILing a Complex Entry.

    We will expand and simplify the product \((2 + i)\left(\cos(3t) + i\sin(3t)\right)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a) FOIL Without Simplifying.

    First, FOIL (expand out) the product \((2 + i)\left(\cos(3t) + i\sin(3t)\right)\) into four terms, keeping any \(i^2\) as is. Which of the following is correct?
    🔗
    • \(2\cos(3t) + 2i\sin(3t) + i\cos(3t) + i^2\sin(3t)\)
    • Correct! The four FOIL terms are: First \(2 \cdot \cos(3t)\text{,}\) Outer \(2 \cdot i\sin(3t)\text{,}\) Inner \(i \cdot \cos(3t)\text{,}\) and Last \(i \cdot i\sin(3t) = i^2\sin(3t)\text{.}\)
    • \(2\cos(3t) + i^2\sin(3t)\)
    • This has the First and Last terms, but FOIL has four terms! The Outer term \(2 \cdot i\sin(3t)\) and the Inner term \(i \cdot \cos(3t)\) are missing.
    • \(2\cos(3t) + 2i\sin(3t) + i\cos(3t) + i\sin(3t)\)
    • Careful with the Last term: it is \(i \cdot i\sin(3t)\text{,}\) and the two factors of \(i\) multiply together to give \(i^2\sin(3t)\text{,}\) not \(i\sin(3t)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (b) Simplify with \(i^2 = -1\).

    Now substitute \(i^2 = -1\) into \(2\cos(3t) + 2i\sin(3t) + i\cos(3t) + i^2\sin(3t)\) and group the terms with an \(i\) separately from the terms without. Which of the following is correct?
    🔗
    • \(\left(2\cos(3t) - \sin(3t)\right) + i\left(2\sin(3t) + \cos(3t)\right)\)
    • Correct! Since \(i^2 = -1\text{,}\) the last term becomes \(-\sin(3t)\text{,}\) which has no \(i\text{,}\) so it joins \(2\cos(3t)\) in the real part. The terms with a single \(i\text{,}\) namely \(2i\sin(3t)\) and \(i\cos(3t)\text{,}\) group into the imaginary part.
    • \(\left(2\cos(3t) + \sin(3t)\right) + i\left(2\sin(3t) + \cos(3t)\right)\)
    • Watch the sign: substituting \(i^2 = -1\) into \(i^2\sin(3t)\) gives \(-\sin(3t)\text{,}\) so the real part is \(2\cos(3t) - \sin(3t)\text{.}\)
    • \(\left(2\cos(3t) - \sin(3t)\right) + i\left(2\sin(3t) - \cos(3t)\right)\)
    • The real part is right, but check the imaginary part: the terms with a single \(i\) are \(+2i\sin(3t)\) and \(+i\cos(3t)\text{,}\) both with plus signs, so the imaginary part is \(2\sin(3t) + \cos(3t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Now we are ready for the example below!
    🔗

    Example 5.5.1. A Harder Example with Complex Eigenvalues.

    Find the general solution of the equation \(\dfrac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = A\mathbf{x}\text{,}\) where \(A\) has eigenvalues \(\lambda_1 = 2 + 3i\) and \(\lambda_2 = 2 - 3i\) with corresponding eigenvectors
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 + i \\ 3 - 2i \end{bmatrix} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 - i \\ 3 + 2i \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    We follow the three steps. Step 1: the complex-valued solution is
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{x}(t) = \mathbf{v}_1 e^{\lambda_1 t} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 + i \\ 3 - 2i \end{bmatrix}e^{(2 + 3i)t}. \end{equation*}
    (Using \(\lambda_2\) and \(\mathbf{v}_2\) also works. Just make sure the eigenvalue and eigenvector go together!)
    🔗
    Step 2: we apply Euler’s formula to the exponential,
    \begin{equation*} e^{(2 + 3i)t} = e^{2t}\left(\cos(3t) + i\sin(3t)\right), \end{equation*}
    so
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{x}(t) = e^{2t}\begin{bmatrix} (2 + i)\left(\cos(3t) + i\sin(3t)\right) \\ (3 - 2i)\left(\cos(3t) + i\sin(3t)\right) \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Each component needs to be FOILed out and simplified, as in the warm-up. The details are in the dropdowns.
    🔗
    Solution 1. Details: FOILing the first component
    This is exactly the warm-up computation:
    \begin{align*} (2 + i)\left(\cos(3t) + i\sin(3t)\right) \amp= 2\cos(3t) + 2i\sin(3t) + i\cos(3t) + i^2\sin(3t)\\ \amp= \left(2\cos(3t) - \sin(3t)\right) + i\left(2\sin(3t) + \cos(3t)\right). \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution 2. Details: FOILing the second component
    Same process, watching the signs from the \(-2i\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} (3 - 2i)\left(\cos(3t) + i\sin(3t)\right) \amp= 3\cos(3t) + 3i\sin(3t) - 2i\cos(3t) - 2i^2\sin(3t)\\ \amp= \left(3\cos(3t) + 2\sin(3t)\right) + i\left(3\sin(3t) - 2\cos(3t)\right). \end{align*}
    Note the term \(-2i^2\sin(3t) = -2(-1)\sin(3t) = +2\sin(3t)\text{,}\) which is real.
    🔗
    🔗
    Putting the FOILed components back in:
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{x}(t) = e^{2t}\begin{bmatrix} \left(2\cos(3t) - \sin(3t)\right) + i\left(2\sin(3t) + \cos(3t)\right) \\ \left(3\cos(3t) + 2\sin(3t)\right) + i\left(3\sin(3t) - 2\cos(3t)\right) \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Now separating the real and imaginary parts:
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{x}(t) = \underbrace{e^{2t}\begin{bmatrix} 2\cos(3t) - \sin(3t) \\ 3\cos(3t) + 2\sin(3t) \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{y}} + i\underbrace{e^{2t}\begin{bmatrix} 2\sin(3t) + \cos(3t) \\ 3\sin(3t) - 2\cos(3t) \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{z}}. \end{equation*}
    Step 3: the general solution is
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{x}(t) = c_1\mathbf{y} + c_2\mathbf{z} = c_1 e^{2t}\begin{bmatrix} 2\cos(3t) - \sin(3t) \\ 3\cos(3t) + 2\sin(3t) \end{bmatrix} + c_2 e^{2t}\begin{bmatrix} 2\sin(3t) + \cos(3t) \\ 3\sin(3t) - 2\cos(3t) \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Once again, there is no \(i\) anywhere in the final answer.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaways.

    • Same three steps: messier eigenvectors don’t change the strategy, only the amount of algebra in step 2.
      🔗
      🔗
    • FOIL each component: multiply each eigenvector entry into \(\cos(qt) + i\sin(qt)\text{,}\) substitute \(i^2 = -1\text{,}\) and group the terms with an \(i\) separately from the terms without.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Only one pair is needed: either eigenvalue-eigenvector pair works, as long as the eigenvalue and eigenvector go together.
      🔗
      🔗
