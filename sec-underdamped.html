    Section 3.3.2 Behavior of Underdamped Solutions

    Observation 3.3.1. Underdamped Solution Form.

    When \(c^2 \lt 4km\text{,}\) the characteristic equation has complex conjugate roots and the system is underdamped. The general solution takes the form:
    \begin{equation*} x(t) = e^{\alpha t}\bigl(c_1 \cos(\omega t) + c_2 \sin(\omega t)\bigr), \end{equation*}
    where \(\alpha \lt 0\) and \(\omega \gt 0\) are determined by \(m\text{,}\) \(c\text{,}\) and \(k\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Notice that this solution is the product of two pieces: an exponentially decaying factor \(e^{\alpha t}\) (with \(\alpha \lt 0\)) and an oscillating trigonometric expression \(c_1 \cos(\omega t) + c_2 \sin(\omega t)\text{.}\) The exponential factor acts as a shrinking envelope that squeezes the oscillations toward zero over time.
    🔗
    To see how \(\alpha\) affects the solution, the graph below compares the pure trig part (no decay) to the full underdamped solution. Move the slider to change the decay rate.

    Remark 3.3.3.

    Notice that the shape of the underdamped solution is very similar to the undamped case from Section 3.2.1: both oscillate and cross the equilibrium position repeatedly. The key difference is that the amplitude is decaying because of the factor \(e^{\alpha t}\) with \(\alpha \lt 0\text{.}\) Physically, the dashpot is continuously removing energy from the system, causing the oscillations to shrink.
    🔗

    Checkpoint 3.3.5.

    By observing the graph above, what can we deduce about how many times the mass passes through the equilibrium position \(x = 0\) as time goes on forever?
    🔗
    • Infinitely many times.
    • Correct! The oscillations never fully stop in finite time. No matter how large \(t\) gets, the curve continues to cross \(x = 0\text{.}\) \(\checkmark\)
    • Exactly once.
    • Look again at the graph. The curve crosses \(x = 0\) many times, not just once.
    • At most once.
    • This is the behavior of overdamped and critically damped systems, not underdamped.
    • Never.
    • The graph shows the curve oscillating back and forth across \(x = 0\) repeatedly.

    Example 3.3.6. How Many Times Does the Mass Cross Equilibrium?

    For an underdamped system, show algebraically that the mass passes through the equilibrium position \(x = 0\) infinitely many times.
    🔗
    Solution.
    This is a homework problem. The argument is very similar to the one in Example 3.2.3 for the undamped case, so reviewing that example first is a good starting point. We already know from the graph above that the answer should be infinitely many times, and on the homework you will justify this algebraically.
    🔗
    Hint: start with \(x(t) = 0\) and use the fact that \(e^{\alpha t} \neq 0\) for all \(t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Now a key idea here was that the exponential decay may make the amplitude very tiny, but it never actually becomes 0. So, even though the amplitude is decaying, we will observe the mass is oscillating back and forth forever.
    🔗

    Observation 3.3.7.

    Even though the mass passes through equilibrium infinitely many times, the damping causes the oscillations to shrink down to zero:
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{t \to \infty} x(t) = \lim_{t \to \infty} \underbrace{e^{\alpha t}}_{\to\, 0} \cdot \underbrace{\bigl(c_1 \cos(\omega t) + c_2 \sin(\omega t)\bigr)}_{\text{bounded}} = 0. \end{equation*}
    So the oscillations never fully stop in finite time, but they shrink to zero in the long run.
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaways.

    • Solution form: Underdamped solutions have the form \(x(t) = e^{\alpha t}(c_1 \cos(\omega t) + c_2 \sin(\omega t))\) with \(\alpha \lt 0\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Decaying envelope: The factor \(e^{\alpha t}\) shrinks the amplitude of the oscillations toward zero. A more negative \(\alpha\) means faster decay.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Equilibrium crossings: The mass passes through \(x = 0\) infinitely many times.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Long-term behavior: \(\displaystyle\lim_{t \to \infty} x(t) = 0\text{.}\) The mass approaches equilibrium but oscillates forever while doing so.
